Most leadership advice assumes there's one right way to manage. There isn't. The managers who feel most confident aren't the ones copying a template — they're the ones leading from an honest picture of their actual strengths.

## Why this matters more than most advice

A management style borrowed from someone else — your old boss, a book, a podcast — will always feel slightly off, because it's not built around how you actually think and communicate. Working with your natural strengths instead of against them is usually the difference between management feeling exhausting and feeling sustainable.

## What the assessment covers

### Communication style
Whether you naturally lead with directness, with questions, or with structure — and how that shows up in your 1:1s and team meetings.

### Decision-making pattern
How you weigh data versus intuition, and how fast you tend to move once you have enough information.

### Conflict approach
Whether you lean toward addressing tension head-on or need more space to think before responding — and how to manage that instinct on a deadline.

### Energy sources
What kind of work actually recharges you, so you can protect time for it instead of letting your calendar fill it in with meetings.

## What you get afterward

A short written profile of your top three leadership strengths, common blind spots that tend to pair with them, and a few concrete ways to lean into your strengths in your next 1:1 or team meeting.

## After the assessment

Most people use their results as a starting point for the New Manager Bootcamp or Signature Coaching — both build directly on your specific strengths rather than a generic curriculum.
