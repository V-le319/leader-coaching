sections:
  - heading: "The shift nobody explains"
    body: "Your job used to be measured by what you produced. Now it's measured by what your team produces — including the parts you didn't touch. That's a harder thing to feel good about, because your fingerprints are less visible on the outcome, and it means your daily work looks completely different: more conversations, more decisions about other people's time, less time at the keyboard. Most new managers try to hold onto their old identity for too long, quietly doing individual work on the side to feel productive. That instinct is normal. It's also the fastest way to become a bottleneck for your team."
  - heading: "Four skills to build first"
    body: "1. Delegation that doesn't boomerang — handing off a task isn't the same as handing off ownership. Give people the outcome you need and the context behind it, then let them choose the method. 2. 1:1s that aren't status updates — use them for the things that don't fit in Slack: how someone's doing, what's blocking them, where they want to grow. 3. Feedback in the moment, not the review — say the small thing early, while it's still small. 4. Deciding without full consensus — ask for input, set a deadline for the decision, and be honest when it's your call to make."
  - heading: "Your first two weeks, in order"
    body: "Skip the urge to change everything immediately. Spend the first two weeks doing three things: meet each person on your team 1:1 to understand how they like to work, learn what \"good\" already looks like on this team before you try to improve it, and agree on how you and your own manager will communicate. Everything else can wait a little longer."
mistakes:
  - "Managing everyone the same way. A new hire and a ten-year veteran need different amounts of guidance."
  - "Avoiding hard conversations to stay liked. The discomfort doesn't go away; it just gets bigger."
  - "Confusing being busy with being effective. A full calendar isn't the same as a team that's unblocked and growing."
