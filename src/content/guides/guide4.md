  - "The hardest part of feedback usually isn't knowing what to say, it's finding the words in the moment, before the conversation drifts into vague or overly soft language that doesn't actually land."

sections:
  - heading: "Why scripts help"
    body: "A script isn't about sounding robotic, it's a starting point so you're not improvising the hardest sentence of the conversation in real time. Once you've said it once, it gets easier to make it your own."
  - heading: "Four conversations, four starting points"
    steps:
      - title: "Performance is slipping"
        text: "\"I've noticed [specific thing] over the last [timeframe]. Can you walk me through what's been going on?\" Lead with the specific pattern, not a general impression, and ask before assuming."
      - title: "Someone needs to hear they're doing well"
        text: "\"I want to call out [specific action], that's exactly the kind of [quality] I want to see more of.\" Specificity is what makes praise land instead of feeling generic."
      - title: "A behavior needs to stop"
        text: "\"When [specific behavior] happens, it affects [specific impact]. I need that to change starting now.\" Direct, specific, and tied to a real consequence, not a vague request to \"do better.\""
      - title: "Someone disagrees with your decision"
        text: "\"I hear that you'd have gone a different way. Here's why I made this call, I'm still open to your take if something changes.\" Acknowledge the disagreement without re-opening the whole decision."
mistakes:
  - "Burying the point in a compliment sandwich. People remember the compliments and forget the actual issue."
  - "Waiting for the \"right moment.\" The right moment is usually just after the thing happens, not weeks later."
  - "Softening it into vagueness. \"Just something to think about\" doesn't tell anyone what to actually change."
