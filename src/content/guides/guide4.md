The hardest part of feedback usually isn't knowing what to say — it's finding the words in the moment, before the conversation drifts into vague or overly soft language that doesn't actually land.

## Why scripts help

A script isn't about sounding robotic — it's a starting point so you're not improvising the hardest sentence of the conversation in real time. Once you've said it once, it gets easier to make it your own.

## Four conversations, four starting points

### 1. Performance is slipping
"I've noticed [specific thing] over the last [timeframe]. Can you walk me through what's been going on?" Lead with the specific pattern, not a general impression, and ask before assuming.

### 2. Someone needs to hear they're doing well
"I want to call out [specific action] — that's exactly the kind of [quality] I want to see more of." Specificity is what makes praise land instead of feeling generic.

### 3. A behavior needs to stop
"When [specific behavior] happens, it affects [specific impact]. I need that to change starting now." Direct, specific, and tied to a real consequence — not a vague request to "do better."

### 4. Someone disagrees with your decision
"I hear that you'd have gone a different way. Here's why I made this call — I'm still open to your take if something changes." Acknowledge the disagreement without re-opening the whole decision.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Burying the point in a compliment sandwich.** People remember the compliments and forget the actual issue.
- **Waiting for the "right moment."** The right moment is usually just after the thing happens, not weeks later.
- **Softening it into vagueness.** "Just something to think about" doesn't tell anyone what to actually change.

## Using this guide

Keep this open the next time a hard conversation is coming up. Adjust the wording to sound like you, but keep the structure: specific, direct, and soon.
